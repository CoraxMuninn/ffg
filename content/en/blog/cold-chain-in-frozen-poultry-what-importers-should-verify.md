A cold-chain statement should be more specific than “kept frozen.” The buyer needs a temperature condition, the points where it applies, and the records required for the order. The storage condition published on this website is -18°C after freezing.

## Map the transfer points

Product may move from freezing to cold storage, from storage to the loading area, into a reefer container, through a terminal, and finally into destination storage. Each transfer is a point to ask about because the product can be outside its normal storage environment.

The [export supply-chain page](/supply-chain) shows this sequence without claiming a fixed port, carrier, or transit time.

## Confirm before loading

Ask the supplier to state:

- the agreed storage temperature;
- the reefer set point for the booking;
- which temperature records will be available;
- whether loading and the container seal will be documented;
- whether an independent pre-shipment inspection is required.

The answers should appear in the order or inspection instructions, not only in a sales message.

## Check the container on arrival

Before unloading, compare the seal identifier with the shipping documents and review any temperature information available to you. Inspect selected cartons against the agreed product specification. If there is evidence of thawing, refreezing, damaged packing, or another non-conformity, follow the claim procedure in the contract and preserve the records.

## Keep product and documents aligned

The packing list and shipment documents should describe the goods actually loaded. The exact document set depends on the destination, so consult the buyer’s broker or import specialist rather than assuming a generic list is sufficient.

For a product- and destination-specific discussion, include the required evidence in the [RFQ](/contact).
