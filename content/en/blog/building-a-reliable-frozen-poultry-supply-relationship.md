A repeat supply programme is not created by a broad promise of “consistent quality.” It starts with a document both parties can use when the next order is placed. For an importer, that means fewer assumptions and a clearer basis for inspection.

## Use one written specification

The reference should name the product, freezing method, grade where relevant, size or weight range, glaze where relevant, storage temperature, packing, and marking. If a point cannot be measured or checked, decide whether it belongs in the specification at all.

When a requirement changes, issue a revised version. Do not rely on email fragments from several orders ago.

## Define the checks with the product

A quality plan should identify the sample, sample size, acceptance criteria, and evidence. Product appearance, size, broken pieces, glaze, defrost odour, temperature, and container-seal records are separate checks. Not every check applies to every poultry product.

Our [quality-control overview](/quality-control) explains these checkpoints without presenting unpublished pass rates or inspection results.

## Keep the document list current

Health, veterinary, origin, Halal, packing, and other documents may be relevant depending on the destination and import channel. Record the required categories with the order and verify them again if the product, port, bank, broker, or regulation changes.

## Flag changes before commercial terms are fixed

Quantity, packing, destination, delivery scope, and document requirements can change. A buyer should communicate them before asking for a final price, and a supplier should confirm what can be addressed before making a commitment.

## Make the first RFQ usable

A useful enquiry includes the product, quantity, destination port, packing or marking, and required documents. That creates a reference for the first order and any later discussion. Review the [product range](/products), then send a defined [request for quote](/contact).
