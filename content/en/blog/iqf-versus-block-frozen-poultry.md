IQF and block frozen describe the physical form of a frozen product. Neither term, by itself, proves grade, food safety, shelf life, or suitability for a market. The choice should follow the buyer’s handling and processing needs.

## IQF: pieces frozen separately

IQF means individually quick frozen. Separate pieces can be sampled across a carton, weighed, counted, portioned, or repacked without thawing one solid block. This is useful when size uniformity or piece-level appearance matters.

A sample should still confirm that the pieces are genuinely separate and match the written specification.

## Block frozen: product frozen together

Block-frozen product is supplied as a solid frozen unit. It may suit a processor that thaws a complete block for production, but it offers less access for piece-level inspection while frozen. Repacking may also require a different handling plan.

## Compare the full offer, not the label

Freezing method is only one commercial variable. Compare:

- declared net weight and any glaze;
- product grade and size requirements;
- pack weight and carton format;
- storage condition and shelf-life evidence;
- inspection and document scope;
- delivery term and total landed cost.

Pricing depends on the product, pack format, quantity, origin, and delivery terms. Compare like-for-like offers rather than assume one fixed premium for either format.

## Storage still matters

Both formats require the storage condition stated in the product specification. For the products listed here, the published condition is -18°C after freezing.
