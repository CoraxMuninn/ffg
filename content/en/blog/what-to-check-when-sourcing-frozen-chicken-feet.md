Frozen chicken feet offers can use the same grade name while describing different products. A buyer should therefore compare the written criteria behind the grade, not the label alone.

## 1. Define Grade A or A+

Write the visual and structural requirements into the specification. The Feiz Food Group product page lists natural white colour, clean nails, no feathers, and no black spots. Project documentation also lists no blood, no contamination, and no off odour after defrost.

These points need a sample method and acceptance rule if they are to be inspected objectively.

## 2. Confirm average piece weight

The published average is 35–55g per piece. Clarify whether the buyer needs an average, a minimum, a calibrated range, or a count-per-kilogram measure; those are not the same requirement.

## 3. Verify IQF form and storage

IQF pieces should be separate rather than fused into one block. The published storage condition is -18°C after freezing. Ask what temperature evidence will be available for the proposed order.

## 4. State the glaze range

The published glaze range is 5–10%. Because glaze affects usable net weight, the order should state how it is measured and what tolerance applies. Do not rely on a verbal description such as “light glaze.”

## 5. Inspect a representative sample

Agree how many cartons and pieces will be selected, who will inspect them, and what happens if the result is outside the specification. A clean-looking studio sample is not evidence for an entire commercial lot.
