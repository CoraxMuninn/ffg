A quality-control list is useful only when it is linked to a product specification and an acceptance rule. Otherwise, terms such as “strict inspection” tell a buyer very little.

## Set the reference

A written specification or approved sample should define the product. For frozen chicken feet, relevant published points include Grade A/A+, 35–55g average piece weight, 5–10% glaze, appearance requirements, and -18°C storage. Those figures do not automatically apply to liver, gizzard, or heart.

## Match each check to a risk

- **Size or weight sampling** checks uniformity against the agreed range.
- **Broken-piece counts** check physical damage in selected cartons.
- **Glaze measurement** checks a declared range where glaze applies.
- **Defrost odour assessment** checks a condition that is not apparent while fully frozen.
- **Temperature records** relate to the agreed storage condition.
- **Container-seal photographs** record the closed container and seal identifier.

## Define the sampling plan

The number of cartons, selection method, acceptance limit, and responsible inspector should be known before the inspection. A result from one convenient carton cannot represent a full lot unless both sides explicitly accept that method.

## Separate company claims from order evidence

A logo, general quality statement, or process diagram is not a lot-specific inspection result. Ask which laboratory, inspection, temperature, or loading records will be available for the actual order.
