International frozen poultry sourcing becomes easier when the enquiry is treated as a controlled procurement process rather than a request for a headline price. Begin with what you can define and verify.

## Write the requirement first

Name the product, quantity, freezing method, destination port, packing, marking, and required documents. Add grade, piece weight, size, or glaze only where they apply to that product. A chicken-feet specification should not be copied onto liver, gizzard, or heart.

## Compare like with like

Two offers are not comparable if one omits glaze, net weight, packing, delivery term, or document scope. Create a comparison sheet and mark every point as confirmed, excluded, or still open.

Questions worth asking include:

- What exact specification will appear on the order?
- Is the product IQF or block frozen?
- What storage condition applies?
- Which documents are included, and who issues them?
- What inspection can be arranged before loading?
- Which Incoterm and delivery scope are being quoted?

## Verify documents for the destination

Do not assume one document list works everywhere. The importer, customs representative, bank, and destination authority may each have requirements. Confirm the current list before commercial terms are finalised. Our [market pages](/markets) are starting points, not regulatory approval or customs advice.

## Agree the inspection scope

If an inspection is needed, define the sample, acceptance criteria, timing, inspector, and reporting format. “Inspection available” is too vague to protect either side.
