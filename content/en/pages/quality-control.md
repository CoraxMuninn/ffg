Frozen poultry quality control is useful only when each check has a clear purpose and is tied to an agreed specification. The checkpoints below show what a buyer may need to define, verify, or request for a lot.

## Product checks

Sample approval establishes a reference before an order proceeds. Size uniformity, broken-piece ratio, and random-carton checks compare the supplied product with that reference. For frozen chicken feet, the published glaze range is 5–10%; that range should not be applied automatically to other products.

A defrost odour check evaluates a condition that cannot be judged while the product remains fully frozen. The method, sample size, acceptance criteria, and responsible party should be agreed rather than assumed.

## Temperature and loading records

The documented storage condition is -18°C after freezing. Temperature records, pre-shipment inspection, loading checks, and photographs of the sealed container are separate forms of evidence. Which records are provided depends on the agreed order and inspection arrangement.

## What buyers should confirm

Before placing an order, ask which checkpoints apply to the product, whether a third-party inspection is required, what evidence will be available, and how any non-conformity will be handled in the commercial documents.

Laboratory details, audit records, and inspection results are order-specific. Ask which evidence will be available for your lot when you [request a quote](/contact), or see where the checks sit in the [export supply chain](/supply-chain).
